THE UNIT CONVERSION ENGINE (CRITICAL) You must be very mindful of how Purchase and Selling unit conversions work to ensure perfect inventory management. Include these fields:
•	Primary Purchase Unit (Dropdown): STRIP, BOTTLE, PACK, JAR, TABLET, CAPSULES, BOX, TUBE, PIECE, VIAL, INJ, SACHET, SPRAY BOTTLE, CAN, UNIT, ampule, drop, packet, pouch, INSURANCE, RESPULE, AMPOULE, GLOVES, INSULIN, AMPULE, VIAL & SMALL BOTTLE, SYRINGE, TIN, CONTAINER, SUPPOSITRIES, ML, INJECTION.
•	Secondary Unit: Will have same variables as the primary purchase unit
•	Purchase Conversion Multiplier
•	Sale Unit: Will have same variables as the primary purchase unit
•	Selling Conversion Multiplier
Example Logic to Implement: If a user adds Pantoprazole 40: 1 Box has 10 Strips, and 1 Strip has 10 Tablets. The Primary Purchase Unit is Box. The Sale Unit is Tablet (or Strip). The system must multiply down so that when 1 Box is purchased, the inventory accurately reflects the correct number of Sale Units (e.g., 100 Tablets). For liquids (e.g., Cough Syrup): Purchase Unit is Bottle, Sale Unit is Bottle. The multiplier is simply 1.
ANOTHER EXAMPLE: Vitamin D3 60K Tablets
•	Packaging: 1 Box contains 10 Strips. 1 Strip contains 4 Tablets.
•	Primary Purchase Unit: Box
•	Secondary Unit: Strip (Purchase Conversion Multiplier = 10)
•	Sale Unit: Tablet (Selling Conversion Multiplier = 4)
Crucial Nuance: The system must support purchasing in EITHER the Primary Unit OR the Secondary Unit. There is no guarantee a full box will be purchased; a vendor might supply 5 loose strips. The backend must be able to calculate total sale units based on which unit was used during the purchase.
